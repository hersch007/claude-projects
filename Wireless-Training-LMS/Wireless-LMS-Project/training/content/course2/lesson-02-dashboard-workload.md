## Overview

The dashboard is the first thing you see when you log in to AMP. For a Jurisdiction reviewer managing a busy queue of wireless facility applications, it is also the primary management tool for your workload. Every project in your jurisdiction -- active, pending, under review, or awaiting action -- is visible here.

Reading the dashboard correctly is not optional. It is the skill that separates reviewers who stay ahead of their Shot Clock obligations from those who miss deadlines and face federal legal consequences. This lesson teaches you how to read the dashboard the right way: every row, every column, every time.

> **Key Point:** Never assess a project's status from a single column. The dashboard is designed to be read as a complete row from left to right. A single green status column does not tell you whether someone owes an action on the next stage. A single blue column does not tell you where the Shot Clock stands. Always read the full row.

## What You See on the Dashboard

### The Jurisdiction Reviewer's View

When you log in as a Jurisdiction reviewer (Client), your dashboard shows **all active projects in your jurisdiction** -- not just projects you personally opened or projects from a specific carrier. This is by design. Jurisdiction oversight is comprehensive. You are responsible for the timeline and status of every application in your jurisdiction, whether you are the primary reviewer or not.

This is different from what an Agent sees:

| User Type | Dashboard View |
|---|---|
| **Client (Jurisdiction)** | All projects in the jurisdiction |
| **Analyst** | All projects in assigned jurisdiction(s) |
| **Agent** | Only projects they personally submitted |

The implication: as a Jurisdiction reviewer, you can see projects that were submitted by any carrier to your jurisdiction. You have full visibility. Agents cannot see each other's projects. Analysts see the same scope as you for their assigned jurisdictions.

### Dashboard Columns: What Each One Tells You

A typical AMP dashboard row contains the following information. Learn what each column means before you make any workload decisions:

| Column | What It Shows | What to Ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Project Name / ID** | Carrier-submitted project identifier | Is this a project I'm familiar with? |
| **Project Type** | New Tower, Colocation, SWF, etc. | What Shot Clock length applies? What components are required? |
| **Carrier / Agent** | Who submitted | Is this carrier known to submit clean applications? |
| **Jurisdiction** | Confirms it routes to you | Should always be your jurisdiction |
| **PIF Status** | Current PIF stage | Has the PIF been approved? Is it stuck? |
| **Component Status** | Status of each required component | Which components are complete, under review, or in revision? |

| **Shot Clock** | Days remaining for Jurisdiction action | How urgent is this? |
| **Last Activity** | Most recent action date | Has anything moved recently? Has it been sitting untouched? |

## Understanding Status Colors From the Reviewer's Perspective

Course 1 introduced the eleven project statuses. This lesson revisits them from the perspective of what each one means **for you as a reviewer** -- what action is required, what can wait, and what cannot.

### Statuses That Require Your Immediate Attention

**Red statuses are the highest priority.** Any project showing a red status means someone has identified a deficiency or required action that has not been resolved. Depending on the specific red status, the required actor may be the Agent, the Analyst, or you.

| Status | Color | What It Means for the Reviewer |
|---|---|---|
| **Revise** | Red | A revision request has been issued -- the Agent must correct and resubmit. Check who issued the request. If it was your Analyst, confirm the deficiency is accurately described. |
| **AIT Revise** | Red | Same as Revise but under the AIT track -- a qualified Analyst must co-approve the AIT's revision determination. |
| **Awaiting Revision** | Red | Additional information has been formally requested. The project is halted until the Agent responds. Monitor for response and confirm the clock implications. |

**Do not let red-status projects age without checking on them.** A revision request that the Agent does not respond to is still your jurisdiction's responsibility to track and act on.

### Statuses That Require Your Active Review

| Status | Color | What It Means for the Reviewer |
|---|---|---|
| **Submitted / In Review** | Blue | The application is under active review. If you are the assigned reviewer, this is in your queue to evaluate. |
| **Saved** | Teal/Cyan | The application has been created but not yet formally submitted. It is visible but not yet actionable for review. |

### Statuses That Require You to Monitor, Not Act

| Status | Color | What It Means for the Reviewer |
|---|---|---|
| **Pending** | Yellow | The project is moving through a workflow step. Something is processing. Monitor to confirm it progresses. |
| **Invoiced** | Orange | Fees have been issued to the Agent. Payment must be received before work can continue. You do not act on this -- the Agent pays, and the system advances. |

### Statuses That Indicate Completed Stages

| Status | Color | What It Means for the Reviewer |
|---|---|---|
| **Approved** | Green | This specific stage has been approved. This does not mean the entire project is done -- it means one stage is complete. |
| **AIT Approved** | Dark Blue | Approved under the AIT track. Qualified Analyst sign-off has been given. |
| **Completed** | Green | The entire project is closed and final. All stages are done. The Official Record is finalized. |

> **Key Point:** Green does not mean done. A project with an Approved PIF may still have components in revision or under review. A project showing Approved at one stage may be in Revise at another. Always look at the full row.

## Prioritizing Your Queue

When you have multiple active projects, you need a consistent method for prioritizing your attention. Use this framework every time you open the dashboard:

### Step 1: Triage Red Statuses First

Any project showing Revise, AIT Revise, or Awaiting Revision gets immediate attention. Open the project, review what was flagged, and determine:
- Is the revision request specific and accurate?
- Has the Agent responded?
- Does the Agent response resolve the identified deficiency?
- Does this affect the Shot Clock? (Revision requests may stop or restart the clock depending on whether they constitute a formal completeness determination.)

### Step 2: Check Shot Clock Values

Sort by Shot Clock days remaining. Any project with fewer than **30 days** remaining on the Shot Clock requires active management -- not passive monitoring. Any project with fewer than **10 days** is urgent.

For SWF applications (60-day clock), 30 days remaining means you are already halfway through your review period. Act accordingly.

> **Visual:** [INSERT IMAGE: Dashboard sorted by Shot Clock column showing urgency levels]
> *Caption: Sort by Shot Clock remaining to immediately surface time-critical projects. Any project under 30 days requires active management.*

### Step 3: Review Blue-Status Applications in Queue Order

Projects in Submitted / In Review status are waiting for your review action. Work through these in order of submission date and Shot Clock days remaining.

### Step 4: Confirm Yellow and Orange Are Moving

Pending and Invoiced projects should be progressing on their own. If a project has been Pending or Invoiced for an unusual length of time, investigate. Fee payment delays can affect the timeline significantly.

### Step 5: Verify Green Is Actually Complete for That Stage

For Approved-status projects, confirm which stage is approved and what remains. A green PIF status does not mean the project is done -- it means you need to look at the components.

## Common Dashboard Misreads

These are the mistakes that lead to Shot Clock violations and compliance failures:

**Misread 1: Looking at the overall status and stopping there.**
The overall status column reflects the most recent major action. It does not show you every stage of a multi-component project. Open the project and review all component statuses, not just the headline.

**Misread 2: Assuming green means no action needed.**
"Approved" at one stage does not mean the project needs no attention. The next stage may be waiting for your review. Check the full project status, not just the summary row.

**Misread 3: Not checking the Shot Clock column when triaging.**
Some reviewers sort by submission date and work chronologically. This is a mistake if newer applications are SWF projects with 60-day clocks while older applications are New Tower projects with 150-day clocks. Always factor in the Shot Clock length and remaining days together.

**Misread 4: Ignoring a red-status project because "the Analyst is handling it."**
Red status projects are flagged. They still require your attention to confirm the revision request is correct, monitor the Agent's response, and ultimately make the next review decision. The Analyst does not close red-status projects on your behalf.

**Misread 5: Treating the dashboard as a filing system instead of a workload tool.**
The dashboard is not a historical archive. It shows you what needs to happen right now. Check it frequently, work it systematically, and use the Shot Clock column as your urgency guide.

## The Shot Clock on the Dashboard

The Shot Clock column is the most critical number on your dashboard. It shows how many days remain for your jurisdiction to take action before the application is deemed approved by federal law.

Key rules for reading the Shot Clock on the dashboard:

- **The number is counting down** -- the lower the number, the more urgent the situation
- **Different project types have different clocks** -- a project showing 45 days remaining on a 60-day SWF clock is more urgent than a project showing 45 days remaining on a 150-day New Tower clock
- **The clock counts toward your jurisdiction's action, not just the review** -- the entire period from submission to final decision must fit within the Shot Clock window
- **AMP tracks the clock automatically** -- but AMP does not automatically stop it. You must take action in AMP to create a clock stop, if applicable

Lesson 5 covers Shot Clock mechanics in detail. For the dashboard, the rule is simple: **any project with a single-digit Shot Clock remaining is a legal emergency for your jurisdiction.**

## Key Takeaways

- The Jurisdiction reviewer sees all active projects in their jurisdiction. This is full oversight visibility -- not limited to projects you personally assigned.
- Read the complete dashboard row for every project. Status color alone is not sufficient to understand what a project needs.
- Red statuses require immediate attention. Do not let red-status projects sit without investigation.
- Green does not mean complete. Approved at one stage does not mean the project requires no further review.
- Use Shot Clock days remaining as your primary urgency signal. Fewer days = higher priority, regardless of submission order.
- Sort and triage your queue every time you log in. Work red statuses first, then Shot Clock urgency, then submission order.
