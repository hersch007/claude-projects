What Google Expects from a Mental Health Website

Google holds therapy and mental health websites to a higher standard — they call this YMYL (Your Money or Your Life). Here's what's currently absent from wanderingfreecounseling.com:

Dedicated Therapist Bio Page
The "Meet Erin" nav link goes to your homepage. Google expects a standalone page establishing credentials, training, and experience — especially for a health site.
Machine-Readable Credentials
Your LPCC license and EMDRIA certification exist on the site, but not in a format Google can read and verify. Without schema markup, your credentials are invisible to search engines.
Local Business Verification Signals
No structured data exists on any page. Google uses this to confirm your business name, address, phone, and hours — all of which affect your local map ranking.
Content Depth on Specialty Pages
Two specialty pages have very thin content. When someone searches "perinatal trauma therapy Ohio," Google needs enough content to determine your page is the best answer.
Blog or Resource Content
Your site has no ongoing content. Therapists who publish educational articles consistently outrank those who don't — Google rewards demonstrated, ongoing expertise.
Consistent Location Signals
Your homepage says "Columbus Ohio" but your office is in Dublin, OH. Every location reference needs to match — across your site, Google Business Profile, and directories.
