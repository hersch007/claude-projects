🀄 American Mahjong Game Rules FAQ

American Mahjong — Rules Directory

Based on the National Mah Jongg League (NMJL) standard. The official card changes annually; purchase the current year's card at nationalmahjonggleague.org.

1. The Tile Set (152 Tiles)

American Mahjong uses a 152-tile set, larger than Chinese sets:

2. Setup & Dealing

Seating

Four players sit at a table: East, South, West, North. East deals first and goes first each hand. Seats rotate clockwise after each hand (East becomes South, etc.).

Building the Wall

All 152 tiles are shuffled face-down and arranged into four walls (two rows of tiles), one per player side. In this digital version the wall is a single shuffled stack.

Dealing

  1. Each player draws 13 tiles.
  2. Any Flower tiles drawn are set aside (exposed to the flower rack) and replaced by drawing another tile.
  3. Jokers are kept in the hand and are wild.

3. The Charleston

The Charleston is a tile-passing ceremony unique to American Mahjong. It occurs before regular play begins.

First Charleston (mandatory)

  1. First Right — pass 3 tiles to the player on your right.
  2. First Across — pass 3 tiles to the player across from you.
  3. First Left — pass 3 tiles to the player on your left.

Second Charleston (optional)

After the First Charleston, each player may agree to continue:

  1. Second Left
  2. Second Across — you may "steal" up to 3 tiles from the blind pass before they go across.
  3. Second Right

Any player may stop the second Charleston before it begins. The second Charleston is optional and all players must agree to continue.

Optional Pass

After all Charleston passes are complete, there is an optional courtesy pass: you may pass 1, 2, or 3 tiles to any one player (and receive the same number back).

Rules: You must pass exactly 3 tiles per pass. You cannot pass Jokers during the Charleston.

Joker rule: Jokers cannot be passed during the Charleston. If you receive one, it stays.

4. The NMJL Card

The National Mah Jongg League card is published each year and lists all valid winning hands. You must match exactly one hand on the card to win. The card changes annually.

Card Categories (typical)

  • 2024 / Year Hands — hands using the digits of the current year
  • Consecutive Runs — e.g., 123 456 789 in one suit
  • Like Numbers — same number in multiple suits (e.g., 1111C 1111B 1111D)
  • 3-6-9 — hands using multiples of 3
  • Singles & Pairs — seven unique pairs (No Jokers allowed)
  • Winds & Dragons — hands built from honor tiles
  • Addition Hands — tile values that sum to a target
  • Any Like Numbers — flexible same-number hands
  • Quints — five of a kind (requires Jokers)

Hand Structure Notation

FF 123 456 789 DD = 2 Flowers + pung 1–3 + pung 4–6 + pung 7–9 + pair Dragons
1111 2222 3333 DD = kong of 1s + kong of 2s + kong of 3s + pair of Dragons (14 tiles)

Each hand on the card specifies exactly 14 tiles and whether Jokers, Flowers, and specific suits are allowed.

Hand Values

Hands are valued at 25¢, 30¢, 35¢, 40¢, 50¢, or 75¢. Higher-value hands are generally harder to achieve. Players pay the winner the face value of their hand.

5. Regular Play

Turn Order

East goes first. Play proceeds counter-clockwise (East → North → West → South → East…).

A Player's Turn

  1. Draw one tile from the wall.
  2. If you draw a Flower, expose it and draw a replacement.
  3. Discard one tile face-up in the center.

Calling a Discard

After any player discards, the other players may call that tile to complete a set:

  • Pung — claim a discard to make a set of 3 (you hold 2 matching tiles)
  • Kong — claim a discard to make a set of 4 (you hold 3 matching tiles)
  • Quint — claim a discard to make a set of 5 (you hold 4 matching or joker-assisted tiles)
  • Mahjong — claim a discard to complete your winning hand

When you call a tile, you expose the completed set and then discard a tile (except when calling Mahjong).

Priority

Mahjong takes priority over any other call. Otherwise, the player closest in turn order gets priority (though in American Mahjong, any player may call for Mahjong regardless of position).

6. Joker Rules

Jokers are the most powerful tiles in American Mahjong.

What Jokers Can Do

  • Substitute for any tile in a set of 3 or more (Pung, Kong, Quint).
  • Cannot be used for singles or pairs unless a specific hand on the card allows it.
  • Can be used in exposed sets.

Joker Stealing

If a Joker is in an exposed set, any player on their turn may swap the Joker out by providing the real tile it represents:

  1. On your draw turn, before discarding, you may steal a Joker from any exposed set by placing the correct tile into that set.
  2. The Joker comes into your hand.
  3. You still must discard a tile this turn.

Joker Restrictions

  • Jokers cannot be discarded (in most rule sets). If you must discard a Joker, it is a dead tile.
  • Jokers cannot be passed during the Charleston.
  • A hand using only Jokers in pairs/singles (where the card says "No Jokers") is invalid.
Example: Exposed set is [3B][3B][JOKER]. A player holding a 3B may swap it in: [3B][3B][3B], taking the Joker into their own hand.

7. Winning — Declaring Mahjong

How to Win

You win when your 14-tile hand exactly matches one of the patterns on the NMJL card. You declare "Mahjong!"

Self-Draw Win

If you draw the tile you need from the wall, declare Mahjong immediately before discarding.

Claiming Win from Discard

When another player discards your winning tile, call "Mahjong!" and show your complete hand.

Payment

  • If you win by self-draw: all three other players pay the full hand value.
  • If you win from a discard: only the player who discarded pays.
  • Joker hands are sometimes double value (consult your house rules).

Dead Hands

A hand is declared dead (you cannot win) if you:

  • Expose tiles that do not match your declared hand pattern.
  • Have the wrong number of tiles.
  • Declare Mahjong with an invalid hand.

A dead hand continues playing (drawing and discarding) but cannot win.

8. Flowers & Seasons

Flower tiles (Plum, Orchid, Chrysanthemum, Bamboo) and Season tiles (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter) are bonus tiles:

  • When drawn, they are immediately placed face-up in your flower rack and you draw a replacement tile.
  • Some NMJL hands require FF (a pair of flowers) — any two flower tiles count as the pair.
  • Flowers cannot be called from the discard pile.
  • Flowers do not count toward your 13-tile hand.

9. Scoring

American Mahjong uses a flat payment system based on hand values printed on the NMJL card:

  • 25¢ — easier hands
  • 30¢, 35¢, 40¢ — intermediate hands
  • 50¢ — difficult hands
  • 75¢ — the most difficult "joker-free" hands

The discarder pays the winner for a discard win. All three players pay for a self-draw win. Some groups play for higher stakes — adjust as desired.
