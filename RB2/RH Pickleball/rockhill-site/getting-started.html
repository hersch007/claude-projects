New player? Start here

You picked a great time to start.

Pickleball is the easiest racquet sport to pick up and one of the hardest to put down. You can genuinely be rallying within your first hour. Here’s your no-stress path in.

5 steps to your first game

Learn the basics (15 minutes)

Court smaller than tennis, paddle and a plastic ball. Games to 11, win by 2. There’s a “kitchen” (no-volley zone) at the net, and you serve underhand. That’s enough to start.

Know what the numbers mean

“I’m a 3.0” refers to skill ratings from 2.5 to 5.0+. Don’t worry about yours yet - most beginners are a 2.5–3.0. (Full breakdown below.)

Grab a paddle (nothing fancy)

A $40–$80 beginner paddle is perfect. Many groups have loaner paddles too - you can play your first few times without buying anything.

Show up to a beginner-friendly session

Open play or a beginner clinic is the easiest way in. See the friendliest courts to start and find the local groups.

Find your people

The community organizes through CourtReserve, Group.Me, and Facebook. We’ve rounded up all the links in Find Players & Groups.

Skill levels

What 2.5 to 5.0 actually means

Ratings help match players for even, fun games. Don’t stress about your number - the friendly move is to under-rate yourself and play up.

🟢 2.5

Brand New

Learning the rules, serving and returning sometimes, getting a feel for the kitchen and scoring. Where nearly everyone starts.

🟢 3.0

Advancing Beginner

You keep rallies going and are learning to “dink” (soft shots at the net) instead of banging every ball. Consistency is next.

🟡 3.5

Solid Intermediate

You sustain rallies, move to the net as a team, and mix soft shots and drives on purpose. Where a huge chunk of club players live.

🟡 4.0

Strong Intermediate

Consistent, places shots, reliable third-shot drop and dinking game. Forces errors and rarely beats itself.

🟠 4.5

Advanced

Few unforced errors, strong control on soft and power shots, smart shot selection, real teamwork. Competitive tournament territory.

🔴 5.0+

Highly Competitive

Elite consistency, power, touch, and strategy. Regional and national tournament players.

FAQ

Every question a new player asks

Is it hard to learn?
No - that’s the magic of it. Most people are rallying within their first hour. The basics take minutes; the strategy takes a lifetime (in a good way).
Do I need my own paddle?
Not to start. Many sessions have loaner paddles, so you can try it for free first. When you’re ready, a $40–$80 beginner paddle is plenty.
Do I need to bring a partner?
Nope. Most open-play sessions rotate partners and opponents, so you can show up solo and play all day. Want to line someone up? Use Find Players & Groups.
What should I wear?
Comfortable athletic clothes and court shoes (not running shoes). Bring water.
What’s “the kitchen”?
The 7-foot no-volley zone at the net. You can stand in it, but you can’t hit the ball out of the air while you’re there - it keeps the game from becoming all smashes.
How much does it cost to play?
It varies by location - some are free or low-cost, others charge for court time. Check the Courts Guide for each spot.
Can kids / seniors / total beginners play?
Absolutely - all of the above, often on the same court. It’s one of the most age-friendly sports there is.
