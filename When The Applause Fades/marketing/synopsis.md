## Short Synopsis — spoiler-free (~120 words)

*For the website book page, Goodreads, or anywhere a reader shouldn't have the ending given away.*

Elliot Brooks has spent five years learning how to be alone well — a widower, a church elder, a father whose daughter is finally finding her footing. His life in Oceanside, North Carolina runs on quiet rhythms he doesn't expect a knock on the door to interrupt.

Nova Hart is a global pop star who has spent years being seen by everyone and known by no one. A two-hour photo shoot at Elliot's house turns into an afternoon she didn't plan for — pie eaten from the tin, a stranger she tells something true.

What starts as an accident becomes a friendship neither of them can explain, and then something neither of them can walk away from — until the whole world finds out, and they have to decide what it's worth to keep.

## Full Synopsis — with spoilers (~450 words)

*For agent/editor submissions, ARC reader briefings, or anywhere the complete arc is useful to know in advance. Contains the ending.*

Elliot Brooks is a widowed cybersecurity CEO and church elder in the small beach town of Oceanside, North Carolina — five years past his wife Sarah's death, still learning to be alone well. His routines are small and specific: Scripture over morning coffee, a spice rack alphabetized except for the paprika, a relationship with his adult daughter Clara that's still finding its footing. None of it prepares him for Nova Hart, a global pop star, showing up at his door for what's supposed to be a two-hour promotional photo shoot.

What Nova finds instead is a house that isn't performing for anyone — pie eaten straight from the tin, a man who doesn't fill silence or chase her attention. She stays longer than planned. She tells him something true. It becomes the first of many.

Their private friendship deepens into something neither of them can name — until a paparazzi photo of Elliot's driveway at dawn turns his house number into the hashtag #WhoIs522 (**Midpoint, Ch. 16**). From there, the story shifts from *can they find each other* to *can they hold what they've found under the weight of the world knowing it.* Reporters camp outside Elliot's house. His standing as a church elder comes under scrutiny. Nova's label and manager pressure her to manage the narrative before it manages her.

The relationship and Nova's long-dormant faith crisis collide hardest in Berlin (**Black Moment, Ch. 38**), where Nova — mid-tour, isolated, and convinced she's cost Elliot more than she can give back — has a panic attack alone in a car. Elliot's response, when she finally reaches him, is the most specific, unrepeatable thing he says in the whole book: not comfort, but something only he could say, rooted in who she actually is beneath the performance.

The resolution unfolds across the final chapters: Elliot's church elders ask him to consider stepping back; he refuses, and flies to Los Angeles to tell Nova the full cost of that choice in person, on a sidewalk outside her studio (**Ch. 51**). Nova, without knowing he's already made his stand, makes her own — naming him on live television, unscripted, undefended (**Ch. 52**). It costs them both exactly what they feared it would, and neither takes it back.

The novel closes not on a grand gesture but on an ordinary one: Elliot and Nova on the boardwalk where their story started, his first kiss, unhurried and certain. The epilogue lands the whole book's thesis in one image — two coffee mugs on a drying rack, months later, unremarkable and chosen. The applause faded. What was left was real.
