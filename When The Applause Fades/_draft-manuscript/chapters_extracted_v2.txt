Meet mature singles in your area. At fifty-five, after five years of widowhood and his grown daughter’s ongoing attempts to “help,” the internet had decided he required help too. The house still held a whisper of coffee from earlier that morning. Beyond the back windows, the ocean moved in slow gray-blue lines beneath an overcast sky. Early April couldn’t decide what season it wanted to be. In another month, Oceanside’s boardwalk would fill with tourists and rental bikes and sunburned families. For now, the town still belonged to the locals. Saturday was uncomplicated—Scripture, coffee, maybe a walk on the beach if the weather behaved. Not camera crews. Not a global pop star standing on his porch. All because he clicked the wrong advertisement while checking stock reports three weeks earlier. Elliot adjusted the cuffs of his shirt as he crossed the foyer, already trying to think of a polite way to survive whatever this experience was supposed to be. The doorbell rang. Too cheerful. Too loud. He opened the door. And there she was. Nova Hart. Until she stepped inside, Elliot had never noticed how much noise the old house made. Not because she was louder than the room. Because nothing about her belonged to rooms this quiet. The woman beside her looked smaller than the version the world projected onto stadium screens. Not less striking. Just more real. A tall man lingered near the porch steps in a dark windbreaker, alert and composed, watching the street with practiced discretion. “We’re clear,” he said. “Thank you, Reid,” she replied, her voice low and calm. Not for the cameras. “Oh,” she murmured. “This is going to be fun.”
