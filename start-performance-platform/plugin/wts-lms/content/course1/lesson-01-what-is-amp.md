## Overview

The Application Management Platform — AMP — is a web-based SaaS platform built by Wireless Tower Solutions (WTS) to manage the permitting and inspection of wireless towers and facilities for local governments.

AMP does not simply organize paperwork. It is a compliance platform: a structured system that enforces regulatory timelines, generates a permanent legal record, and guides every user through a defined workflow from the moment an application is created to the moment the permit is closed.

Before AMP, local governments received wireless facility applications through a combination of email, paper submissions, fax, and in-person document drops. Each method created a different problem: missing documentation, no audit trail, no standardized format, no deadline tracking, and no single source of truth. When disputes arose — and in wireless permitting, they frequently do — there was often no verifiable record of what was submitted, when, or who reviewed it.

AMP was built to solve all of that.

> **Key Point:** AMP replaces fragmented, multi-channel application processes with a single, structured, fully online workflow. Every action is logged. Every deadline is tracked. Every document is stored. The result is a process that is faster, more compliant, and fully defensible under federal law.

## The Problem AMP Solves

### What Came Before AMP

Before a platform like AMP exists, wireless facility permitting at the local government level typically involves:

- **Email submissions** — applications arrive as email attachments with no tracking, no receipt confirmation, and no audit trail
- **Paper applications** — forms received by mail or in person, manually filed, vulnerable to loss or damage
- **Manual tracking** — staff use spreadsheets or calendar reminders to track application status and deadlines
- **No standardized format** — each carrier submits differently; Jurisdictions must interpret and reformat inconsistent data
- **No automated deadline tracking** — federal Shot Clock requirements must be tracked manually, creating legal risk when deadlines are missed
- **No single system of record** — when disputes arise, there is no authoritative source for what was submitted, what was reviewed, or what was decided

The result is a process that is slow, inconsistent, difficult to audit, and legally vulnerable.

### What AMP Provides Instead

AMP replaces all of these manual processes with a single, structured system:

- Applications are submitted online through a standardized form (the PIF) — no email, no paper
- Every document is uploaded directly into the project — no attachments floating in inboxes
- Status tracks exactly where every project stands and who needs to act next
- The Shot Clock is tracked automatically — the dashboard shows how many days remain for every active project
- Every action is logged automatically — creating the permanent Official Record required by federal law
- All communication between Agent, Analyst, and Jurisdiction happens inside AMP — no side conversations in email

## The Regulatory Context

To understand why AMP matters, you need to understand the regulatory environment it operates in.

### Federal Telecommunications Law

The Telecommunications Act of 1996 — and the FCC regulations that followed — fundamentally changed how local governments interact with wireless carriers. Key provisions relevant to AMP:

**Local governments must act — but cannot unreasonably deny.** Under federal law, local governments have the right to regulate the placement, construction, and modification of wireless facilities. However, they cannot unreasonably discriminate between carriers, cannot prohibit wireless service in their jurisdiction, and cannot deny applications based on radio frequency (RF) emissions concerns where the carrier has demonstrated FCC compliance.

**Decisions must be in writing and supported by a record.** Any local government decision on a wireless facility application — approval, denial, or conditional approval — must be supported by a written record. A verbal decision or a decision with no documentation behind it has no legal standing.

**The Official Record requirement.** Federal law requires local governments to maintain a complete record of all communications and decisions related to wireless facility applications. This is what AMP's Official Record fulfills automatically.

### The Shot Clock

Among the most consequential provisions of federal telecom regulations is the Shot Clock — a defined period within which local governments must act on a wireless facility application:

- **Standard applications** (New Tower, Colocation, Modification): **150 days**
- **Small Wireless Facility (SWF) — single site**: **60 days**
- **Small Wireless Facility (SWF) — batch up to 25 sites**: **90 days**

If the local government does not act within the Shot Clock window, the application is **deemed approved by operation of federal law** — the carrier gains the right to build without waiting for local approval. This is not a procedural penalty; it is a federal legal consequence.

AMP tracks the Shot Clock automatically for every active project. The number of days remaining appears on the dashboard for every project under active Jurisdiction review. Before AMP, this tracking was done manually — and missed deadlines had the same legal consequences.

> **Key Point:** The Shot Clock is federal law. It cannot be waived, extended, or ignored. AMP makes it visible and trackable. Without AMP, missing a Shot Clock deadline is an easy mistake to make — with AMP, it is a deliberate choice to ignore information on the dashboard.

### What This Means for Every AMP User

Every user in AMP — Agent, Analyst, or Jurisdiction — is operating within this federal regulatory framework. The rules in AMP are not arbitrary software requirements. They reflect federal legal requirements:

- The Official Record exists because federal law requires it
- The Shot Clock is tracked because federal law mandates it
- Approval authority is exclusively held by the Jurisdiction because federal law places it there
- Certified documents are required because health, safety, and welfare regulations demand them

## The Five Core Areas of AMP

AMP is organized into five interconnected areas. Understanding each area — and how they connect — is the foundation for everything else in this course.

### Core Area 1: Projects

In AMP, every wireless facility application is organized as a **project**. A project is the central unit of work in the system. Everything — forms, documents, communications, status, fees, and approvals — lives inside a project.

A project contains:
- All information entered by the Agent (PIF, component forms)
- All supporting documents uploaded during the application
- The complete review history — every action, every comment, every decision
- All communications between Agent, Analyst, and Jurisdiction
- Fee and invoice records

Projects move through defined lifecycle stages from creation through final inspection and closure. The current stage is always reflected in the project's status.

> **Visual:** [INSERT IMAGE: AMP project detail view showing the project information panel with lifecycle stage indicator]
> *Caption: Every application in AMP is organized as a project. The project contains everything — from initial submission through final inspection.*

### Core Area 2: The Dashboard

The Dashboard is the first screen every user sees after logging in. It provides a real-time view of every project the user has access to, organized in rows with status columns, Shot Clock values, and last activity timestamps.

The dashboard is not just a list — it is a decision-making tool. Every color, every column, and every value tells you something specific about the state of a project and what needs to happen next.

Different users see different projects on the dashboard based on their role:
- **Agents** see only their own projects
- **Analysts** see all projects in their assigned jurisdiction(s)
- **Clients (Jurisdictions)** see all projects in their jurisdiction

### Core Area 3: The Project Information Form (PIF)

The Project Information Form is the mandatory starting point for every project in AMP. Nothing begins without it.

The PIF collects the foundational information that defines the entire project:
- What type of application this is (New Tower, Colocation, SWF, etc.)
- Where the project is located and which Jurisdiction reviews it
- Who is submitting and who the carrier is
- What the project description is

The PIF's project type selection is the most critical single field in the entire platform. It determines which components are required, what fees apply, which regulatory pathway the project follows, and how the Shot Clock is calculated.

### Core Area 4: Components

After the PIF is approved, AMP activates the **components** required for the project type. Components are the structured forms and document collections that provide all the technical, legal, and engineering data needed for review.

AMP has four components:

| Component | Full Name | When Required |
|---|---|---|
| **PIF** | Project Information Form | ALL projects — always the first step |
| **ATF** | Add Tower Form | When adding a new tower site to the WTS database |
| **NWF** | New Wireless Facility | New Tower projects only |
| **TWF** | Tower/Wireless Facility Registration | When tower data is not current in the WTS database |
| **NCM** | New Colocation/Modification | **ALL project types — no exceptions** |

Components collect the specific technical and legal data that Analysts and Jurisdictions review to make approval decisions. Lesson 5 covers each component in full detail.

### Core Area 5: Workflow and Status

Every project and every component in AMP has a **status** — a color-coded indicator of where things stand and what needs to happen next. Status is what makes the dashboard readable at a glance.

There are eleven project statuses in AMP. Each has a specific color, meaning, and required action:

| Status | Color | General Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Draft | None | Being created — not yet submitted |
| Saved | Teal/Cyan | Saved but not yet submitted |
| Submitted / In Review | Blue | Under active review |

| Invoiced | Orange | Fees issued — payment required |
| Revise | Red | Corrections required immediately |
| AIT Revise | Red | AIT track — corrections required |

## AMP as the System of Record

### What "System of Record" Means

When AMP is described as the **system of record**, it means that AMP is the authoritative, legally recognized source for all information about every wireless facility application. Not email. Not shared drives. Not paper files. AMP.

The Official Record in AMP is:
- **Automatic** — created continuously as users take actions
- **Complete** — every field, every document, every communication, every decision
- **Permanent** — nothing can be deleted or overwritten once submitted
- **Attributed** — every entry is linked to the specific authenticated user who made it
- **Timestamped** — every action has a recorded date and time

### Why It Matters

For local governments, the Official Record is the legal foundation for every approval or denial decision. If a carrier challenges a Jurisdiction's decision, the Official Record in AMP is the evidence that decision was made correctly and for documented reasons.

For Agents and carriers, the Official Record proves that applications were submitted on time, completely, and exactly as submitted — protecting them if a Jurisdiction claims otherwise.

For WTS and Analysts, the Official Record documents their advisory role — showing that they reviewed for completeness and flagged issues appropriately.

> **Key Point:** All communication about an application must happen inside AMP. Phone calls, emails, and conversations outside the platform create information that is not part of the Official Record — and therefore does not legally exist as part of the application process.

## How the Five Core Areas Connect

These five areas are not independent — they are one system:

1. The **Agent** creates a project and completes the **PIF**
2. The **PIF** defines the project type, jurisdiction, and scope
3. AMP activates the required **components** based on the PIF
4. The **Agent** completes and submits the components
5. The project appears on the **dashboard** with its current **status**
6. The **Analyst** reviews the application and the **Jurisdiction** makes decisions
7. **Status** updates in real time as actions are taken
8. The **Shot Clock** counts down — visible on the dashboard — until the Jurisdiction acts
9. Every action writes to the **Official Record** automatically
10. When the project is approved, closed, and final inspection complete — **status becomes Completed**

## Why AMP Matters — By User Type

### For Jurisdiction Users (Clients)

AMP gives local government staff the tools to manage a complex, regulated workload with confidence:
- Every application arrives in a standardized format — no more interpreting incomplete or inconsistent submissions
- Required documents are clearly defined and checked at submission — reviewers work from complete packages
- The Shot Clock is tracked automatically — deadline violations are visible before they happen
- The Official Record documents every decision — providing legal protection if applications are challenged
- Applications are visible across the jurisdiction — no single reviewer is the only person who knows a project exists

### For Agent Users (Carrier Representatives)

AMP provides Agents with a clear, consistent path to approval:
- The structured PIF ensures nothing is forgotten at the time of submission
- Status updates are visible in real time — Agents always know where their application stands
- The Official Record proves submissions were made on time and correctly — protecting Agents if a Jurisdiction fails to act
- The Shot Clock is visible to Agents — giving them a legal basis to escalate if a Jurisdiction misses its deadline
- All revision requests are formal and documented — Agents know exactly what needs to be corrected and can prove they responded

### For Analyst Users (WTS Staff)

AMP allows WTS Analysts to support multiple jurisdictions efficiently:
- Dashboard view covers all projects across assigned jurisdictions — nothing falls through the cracks
- Role-based access means Analysts can review without being able to make final approval decisions — preserving proper authority
- All communications are inside the platform — Analysts' advisory role is documented in the Official Record
- Completeness review is supported by the structured format — required fields and documents are visible and checkable
